Extract the smallest set of broad, profile-visible experiences that should be scored after retrieval.
Traits rank candidates; they are not must-haves, and satisfying a subset can be enough. Preserve every
independent recruiter judgment in the JD, but do not add traits to reach a count.

SELECTION
1. Read the full JD. Ignore optional, preferred, bonus, plus, nice-to-have, and ideally material.
2. Use explicit candidate evidence: prior work, experience, contributions, track record, or a required degree,
   credential, scientific background, or regulated-work background. Recurring
   responsibilities may supply a trait when they define the role's core system, method, or specialty.
   Knowledge, comfort, familiarity, fluency, and generic engineering ability only support a retained trait.
3. Compare every proposed pair. Merge traits that restate each other at different levels or would normally be
   proved by the same prior experience. Keep two only when a candidate can be strong in one and weak in the
   other and that difference would materially change ranking. Separate sentences, duties, stages, projects,
   or quotes do not by themselves create separate traits.
4. Stop when the retained traits make the remaining required work plausible. Strong evidence for a parent
   subsumes its ordinary implementation, quality, optimization, debugging, and delivery work.

HIERARCHY
- A recognized system, domain, technical specialty, or scientific specialty is one trait. Do not split its
  subsystems, tools, stages, outputs, or quality goals. Keep a broad systems foundation or cross-cutting discipline separately
  only when it is explicitly required and the primary work does not already establish it.
- Preserve material specialty, scale, latency, reliability, security, or unusually broad role-specific
  lifecycle scope on its parent. Generic end-to-end ownership is not a trait.
- Role aliases, feeder jobs, and generic title history belong to sourcing. Audience context is not a trait by
  itself, but retain it on interface or abstraction design when it changes the technical design problem.
- Preserve distinct technical crafts in a hybrid role when each has explicit prior evidence or a recurring
  body of work. Do not join independent crafts merely because they occur in the same engagement. Keep a
  role-defining technical partnership or field specialty separate from an independent implementation craft,
  and preserve an explicitly required prototype-to-production span on that implementation trait.
- Preserve a required background, degree, credential, clearance, or equivalent career route separately when
  it can vary independently from work experience. Alternatives in one qualification sentence form one
  background trait; never split the degree from its equivalent-experience route or rewrite `or` as `and`.
- For alternative technical deliverables, never select only one branch. Name a neutral shared parent, or keep
  the alternatives with `or` when the distinction matters.
- Field or operations work yields one end-to-end operational trait spanning setup, operation, and completion.
  Preserve integrated hardware and software scope when explicit. Do not add individual test, support,
  configuration, diagnosis, or repair duties. Add another engineering method only when prior track record in
  it is required independently of the operational or background routes. A method named in responsibilities
  and as one alternative within a combined background route stays absorbed rather than becoming another trait.
  For a field or operations role, recurring duties alone never create a second engineering-method trait.
- Keep formal design or analysis methods separate from an end-to-end build lifecycle, and original method or
  tool contributions separate from broad domain practice, when each is explicit and independently scoreable.
- Keep external interface or abstraction design separate from the underlying system only when both are
  defining work.
- For enterprise IT, keep required platform breadth across identity, endpoints, software lifecycle, access,
  and networking as one trait. Keep AI-agent and self-service workflow automation as another when defining;
  security and human oversight are scope on it. Generic IT title history and company stage are not traits.
- For data platforms, keep the concrete entity or modeling problem, production data movement with workflow
  orchestration and external integration, and independently required data access controls as separate traits.
- For growth engineering, combine full-stack growth work and funnel instrumentation; keep rigorous product
  experimentation or A/B testing separately. Developer audience and metric ownership are scope, not traits.
- For applied scientific roles, keep domain analysis and original method, tool, or research-pipeline
  contributions. AI applications or datasets are scope unless the JD requires independent prior evidence.
- For customer-facing technical roles, account lifecycle and customer-facing title history belong to sourcing
  or business context. Keep only independently provable technical solution building as a trait.

NORMALIZATION
Write each trait as concise prior experience a profile could prove, one level above the JD's wording. Drop
empty modifiers such as `hands-on`, `professional`, `proven`, `demonstrated`, `strong`, or `deep`; name the
experience directly in plain recruiter language. Keep only technical scope that changes ranking, such as
distributed, low-latency, or large-scale. Drop company and product nouns, exact numbers, languages,
frameworks, vendors, command-line skills, and implementation tools. Never infer candidate experience from a customer's system, domain, failure, or
problem. Never emit soft skills; generic ownership, delivery, documentation, debugging, troubleshooting,
fault isolation, repair, account ownership, adoption, expansion, metric ownership, business outcomes,
stakeholder management, company familiarity, level, years, seniority, location, authorization, or
compensation. Observability tools and routine operability are supporting evidence for a production-systems
trait unless observability itself is the role's defining specialty.

Every trait needs one exact contiguous `evidence_quote` from candidate-facing requirements,
responsibilities, or recurring-work sections. Copy one contiguous JD substring; never concatenate separate
sentences or bullets. Choose the quote that most directly supports the parent capability, not one that proves
only a supporting qualifier. A trait claiming lifecycle scope needs a contiguous quote that names that
lifecycle; otherwise remove the scope. The quote supports why the parent belongs; the trait states the
transferable experience. `selection_reason` is one short decision summary explaining why the trait
independently changes ranking and which subordinate details it absorbs. It cannot add a material qualifier
absent from the trait. Order traits most defining first. Do not return scratch work.
