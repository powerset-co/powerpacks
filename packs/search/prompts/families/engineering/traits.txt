Extract the 1-6 facts a recruiter should score from a candidate's profile after retrieval.
Most engineering roles need 3-5 traits. Fewer accurate traits beat padded ones.

A trait must be both:
- needed to do this job, from required qualifications or the role's recurring technical work; and
- visible in a profile as prior work, a technical specialty, a differentiating tool, or a credential.

SELECTION
1. Read the entire JD. List its distinct recurring technical outputs and explicit required
   technical backgrounds before writing traits.
2. Ignore optional, preferred, bonus, plus, nice-to-have, and "ideally" material. When a sentence
   mixes required work with an optional qualifier, keep only the required work.
3. Keep the main technical outputs and every genuinely mandatory technical qualification. Do not
   turn every responsibility, product feature, or example into its own trait.
4. Compare the finished traits pairwise. Use one umbrella or its parts, never both. If one profile
   role or project would prove two traits, merge them or keep the more defining one.

NORMALIZATION
- Write capabilities as past work a profile could show: "Built and operated high-volume data
  pipelines" or "Took flight hardware through qualification testing."
- Translate the hiring company's product nouns into the transferable engineering work. Examples
  illustrate one capability; they are not cumulative prior-work requirements.
- Preserve meaningful scale, latency, reliability, or lifecycle scope, but generalize exact numbers:
  "millions of requests" becomes "large-scale" and "in 10 ms" becomes "real-time."
- Preserve every alternative qualification route. For X or Y, keep the word or in one trait. Never
  convert or, and/or, "one of," or equivalent experience into a list the candidate must satisfy.
- Required knowledge becomes a background noun phrase such as "Distributed-systems background,"
  never an unsupported claim that the person built or designed it.
- A differentiating language or tool may be a `tool` trait when the JD explicitly requires deep
  experience in it. Drop generic tech-stack inventories and tools that are merely optional.
- A required degree, license, or research record may be a `background` trait. Drop level, years of
  experience, seniority words, location, work authorization, and compensation.

NEVER A TRAIT
- communication, ownership, autonomy, curiosity, judgment, quality, speed, collaboration, passion,
  culture, work style, or other unmeasurable qualities;
- the hiring company's industry, customers, mission, reputation, or team history;
- a future aspiration, a posting title by itself, or a sentence a profile cannot prove;
- optional evidence promoted because it sounds impressive.

Every trait needs one exact contiguous `evidence_quote` from candidate-facing requirements,
responsibilities, or recurring-work sections. The quote proves why the trait belongs; the trait
rewrites it into concise profile evidence. About/Why-us copy and the posting title are not evidence.
Order traits from most defining to least defining.

Return strict JSON only:
{"traits":[{"trait":"","kind":"capability|background|tool","evidence_quote":""}]}
