Use the supplied channel facts when deciding network worth. Email-backed, genuinely person-directed correspondence biases toward yes even when sparse, old, academic, personal, or plausibly important professionally; reserve no for automated mail, broadcasts, transactions, or unengaged cold spam. For phone-message context, repeated or clearly two-way personal or professional conversation is yes, while a bare phone number, sparse context, or an uncertain one-sided exchange is weak evidence and may be maybe; automated service traffic or obvious spam is no. When email and phone-message context are both present, a real relationship on either channel wins and noise on one must not erase genuine correspondence on the other. When the source remains unclear, judge only the supplied messages and identifiers and prefer maybe over inventing a relationship.
