state.dossier gives two contact records, A and B, that carry the same name, each with the facts and message samples written from the owner's own messages with that contact. There is no other source. Most same-name records are one person seen through two addresses, but a common name can belong to two different people the owner knows. Decide from the facts. ONE PERSON when the two sides fit one life: the same or a related field, employer, city, circle of people or topic; a work address and a personal address; one job after another in the same line of work; or one side that is nearly empty, which argues nothing. TWO PEOPLE when the two sides describe unrelated lives: different professions or industries with nothing connecting them (a tax director and a doctor, a startup CEO and a real-estate agent), different cities with nothing connecting them, or different relationships to the owner (a friend and a stranger sending cold outreach, a colleague and a vendor). No dates are needed for that: unrelated lives under a common name are two people. The rarer the name, the stronger the difference must be before the records are split. NOT A PERSON when either record is a shared mailbox, team, automated sender, event or business. Return the probability that A and B must be kept apart: they are two people, or one of them is not a person.
